# Niche playbook

## Culture
Culture content travels when it explains the "why" behind something people have seen but never understood: a ritual, a word, a festival, a fashion revival. Anchor every claim in a source, name the community you are describing, and let insiders speak rather than speaking over them. Avoid flattening a culture into a stereotype or treating it as exotic. Timely hooks: festivals, anniversaries and viral moments, with context the news clips leave out. Formats that work: "the story behind", myth versus reality, one object explained in 30 seconds.

## Books
BookTok and Bookstagram reward emotion over summary: say how a book made you feel, who it is for, and one line or moment that sells it, without spoilers. Comparison hooks work ("if you loved X, read Y"), as do tier lists, "books that live rent-free in my head" and reading-challenge updates. Quote briefly and credit the author and the edition; never paste long passages. Show the physical book: covers, annotated pages and shelf shots are the visual language of the niche. Check publication dates and author names before stating them.

## Cinema
Film audiences punish spoilers: flag them on screen and in the caption, and keep them out of the first line and the thumbnail. Hooks that work: a hidden detail explained, a director's technique broken down, a hot take on a divisive film, or "films to watch if…" lists. Box office, release dates, awards and cast facts must come from the research dossier; they change quickly. Don't reuse trailer or film footage: describe the shot, use your own B-roll or stills you are licensed to use, and credit the film and studio.

## Comedy
Comedy lives on timing: land the setup in the first two seconds and the punchline before the viewer can swipe. Relatable observation beats random: "things every X does" and exaggerated POV sketches travel furthest. Keep captions short so they don't give away the joke. Punch up, not down: never make identities, disabilities or tragedies the joke. Topical jokes need the research to be current, and a joke about a real person must not state false facts about them. A loopable ending (the last beat sets up the first) boosts rewatches.

## Sports
Sports content peaks around fixtures: preview before the match, react right after, analyse the next day. Stats, scores, transfer fees and records must come from the research dossier, dated, because they go stale by the hour. Broadcast footage and league logos are rights-protected: use your own footage, stills you can license, diagrams and on-screen stats instead. Hooks that work: a stat that reframes a narrative, a tactical breakdown in 30 seconds, "the moment that decided the game", and fan debates such as GOAT arguments. Keep rivalry banter good-natured and never target individual fans.

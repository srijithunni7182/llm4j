# Hook formulas

## Curiosity gap
Open a loop the viewer needs closed: "Nobody tells you this about X…", "I was doing X wrong for years." Deliver the payoff later in the post, never in the first line. The gap must be specific — vague teasers read as clickbait.

## Contrarian take
Challenge a belief the audience holds: "Stop doing X. Do Y instead." Works when you can back the claim with a concrete reason or a verifiable fact. A contrarian hook without proof damages trust.

## Transformation / before-after
"I tried X for 30 days. Here's what changed." Promise a visible change and show the before state first. Keep timelines honest — only claim what actually happened.

## Specific number list
"3 mistakes that kill your X" or "5 tools I use daily for X." Odd, small numbers feel digestible. Each item must earn its place; cut filler items.

## Direct callout
Name the viewer: "If you're a student who hates mornings, watch this." Self-identification makes people stop scrolling because the content is clearly for them.

## Pattern interrupt
Start mid-action or with an unexpected visual or sound. For video, the first frame should already be interesting — no logos, no "hey guys".
